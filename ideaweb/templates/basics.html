1. An alpha is a vector of weights, every day

BRAIN evaluates your expression for every stock each day. Positive values are bought, negative values are sold short, and the platform scales everything to a fixed book. rank(x) turns any number into its position in the cross-section (0 = lowest, 1 = highest); after neutralization the lowest ranks become shorts. Below: the five stocks rank(-ts_sum(returns, 5)) likes least - last week's biggest winners.

2. Know the distribution each operator produces

rank gives uniform 0..1. zscore gives mean 0, sd 1, and tails survive. ts_rank is the position within the stock's own history. Multiplying a 0..1 rank by an unbounded quantity lets the unbounded one dominate. winsorize clips the tails.

{{ b.l2 | safe }}

3. The four numbers BRAIN judges

  • Sharpe = mean daily PnL / std daily PnL x sqrt(252): consistency of profits.
  • Turnover = share of the book traded per day.
  • Returns = annualised PnL / half the book.
  • Fitness = Sharpe x sqrt(|Returns| / max(Turnover, 0.125)).

Below 12.5% turnover the divisor stops shrinking: smoothing a slow signal further cannot raise fitness; it only lowers returns. Above 12.5%, cutting turnover raises fitness directly. For the fast signal below, decay cuts turnover and lifts fitness; for the slow one it does nothing useful. The Lab's diagnosis applies exactly this rule.

{{ b.l3 | safe }}

4. Neutralization: compare with peers, not with the market

Without neutralization an alpha can just be a bet on one sector. group_rank(x, subindustry) and the neutralization setting remove those common bets, so only stock-specific skill remains.

Each planted truth matches an idea card. Notice revision breadth: rung 1 has a good Sharpe but fails fitness (too much turnover); rung 2 (a month of smoothing - an economic choice, analysts update monthly) passes. That is climbing the ladder, not parameter fishing.

Volume itself is not the problem. BRAIN expects 1000+ simulations - that is how you learn the data. The problem is picking the best of many variants of one idea and believing it. Simulate a lot across ideas and datasets, stay disciplined within one idea (a ladder of 2-4 rungs, about 10 tries), and let the journal count every try so the gate can deflate the Sharpe honestly.
